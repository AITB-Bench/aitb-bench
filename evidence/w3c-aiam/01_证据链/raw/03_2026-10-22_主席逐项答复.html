Hello all,

Thank you, Yasha, Silvio, Xinghua and Jeongho. The call said I would 
answer each reply here, so here are my answers in one message.

So far Yasha, Xinghua and Jeongho support option 1. Yasha replied to 
the text as first circulated, and Xinghua and Jeongho also support the 
addition below. No one has preferred option 2 or another document. 
Silvio asked three questions before taking a position, and I answer 
them below.

A change to the text

Xinghua proposed one sentence for §2, and Jeongho supports it. I will 
add it to v1.1, after the sentence "Moving a deliverable to a later 
revision is a Group decision under §9.":

  "Where the revision a deliverable names is superseded by one that
  changes something the deliverable asserts, the Group re-examines that
  deliverable as a decision under §9, rather than leaving it pinned to a
  text that no longer says what it said."

§13 gains this line:

  "Where a later revision changes something a deliverable asserts, the
  Group re-examines that deliverable (added during the call for
  consensus)."

Nothing else in the text changes. Under §11, I am stating 2 November as 
the review period for this addition, so the call still closes then. 
Yasha, if the addition changes your view, please say so. If anyone 
objects to the addition, or wants more time to consider it, please say 
so on this list and I will extend the call.

Silvio's questions

1. Requirements the pinned revision does not meet. The use-case 
catalogue is not limited to what -03 covers. Where a use case needs 
something the pinned revision does not provide, I will propose that the 
catalogue record it as a gap against that revision. As I read §3, a 
conformance criterion tests only what the pinned revision or one of the 
Group's profiles states, so a gap would not count as a failure for any 
implementation. The Group settles that when it reviews the conformance 
pack. A profile can add interoperability guidance but not new protocol 
elements, so a gap that needs a new element stays recorded as a gap. As 
the draft's author, I can be asked to take a gap up in a later 
revision, and I will report on this list what happens to each one. 
Moving any deliverable to a later revision would still be the Group's 
decision.

2. Where a requirement comes from. I will propose that the conformance 
pack mark each criterion either with the section of the pinned revision 
it comes from or with the Group profile that adds it, so that a report 
shows which is which. Jeongho asks for the same separation. The Group 
decides the structure when it reviews the pack.

3. Independent assessment. As the call says, anyone may propose another 
participant to assess the outcome. To record that for this amendment, I 
invite proposals on this list by 27 October. If one participant is 
proposed, agrees to do it, and no one objects on this list by 27 
October, that participant will assess the outcome. Otherwise I will ask 
the W3C Community Development Lead to assess it or to name someone who 
will. In either case I will not confirm the result until the assessment 
is posted here, and I will abide by it.

Xinghua's question on §3

§3 lists the crosswalk regimes as "GDPR Article 17, the EU AI Act 
(Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), NIST AI RMF 1.0, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 
ISO/IEC 42001:2023, and comparable regimes (HIPAA, PIPEDA, PIPL, 
LGPD)". I read the four in brackets as examples, so medical-device 
rules such as EU MDR, 21 CFR Part 820, ISO 13485 and NMPA registration 
review count as comparable regimes too. §5 also lists regulated-data 
handling in the catalogue, and a regulated-data profile as later work. 
On that reading §3 needs no change. If anyone reads §3 differently, 
please say so by 2 November, and I will put the question to the Group. 
Otherwise I will record it as my reading as Chair, which the Group can 
revisit at any time. Thank you also for the retention entry. I will 
answer it with the other use cases in a separate message.

Jeongho's second note

Your first and third notes are answered above. On the second, -03 
specifies the custodial profile, and says that a profile in which the 
holder keeps the keys is possible under the same cell shape but outside 
that revision's scope. Whether the baseline profile and the test 
vectors should avoid assuming that the operator holds the keys is a 
question for the baseline profile, and I will list it as an open item 
there. It does not change the charter text.

Regards,
Russell Jackson
