Hello all,

This is a call for consensus on a charter amendment that concerns my 
own work, so I will declare my interest first. I chair this Group as an 
individual. I am also the author of the Internet-Draft the charter 
references and maintain an implementation of it. The decision is the 
Group's. Participation does not imply endorsement of any 
implementation, including mine.

On 26 August I wrote to this list:

  https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-ai-agent-memory-interop/2026Aug/0002.html

  "the Group's normative reference is not settled -- the Independent
  Submission concluded its consideration in July, and I have said I will bring
  an amendment to re-point the reference, for the Group's review and
  consensus, once the work now forming in the IETF produces a citable
  document."

That has now changed. The IETF chartered the Agent Communication 
Protocols (agentproto) Working Group on 8 October 
(https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/charter-ietf-agentproto/). Its 
charter defines the dialog context it works on and says that context 
"does not include the content exchanged with AI models, such as 
conversation memory, retrieved documents, or prompts." That work is 
therefore not chartered to produce a memory document for this Group to 
cite.

The charter's reference, draft-saihm-memory-protocol, continues as an 
individual Internet-Draft. Revision -03 was posted on 11 October:

  https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-saihm-memory-protocol/03/

I see two options and would like the Group's view on both.

1. Keep and pin (the text below). The charter keeps 
draft-saihm-memory-protocol as its normative reference, describes it as 
an individual Internet-Draft that is a work in progress, not adopted by 
an IETF working group and not an IETF standard, and has each 
deliverable name the revision it was written against. Moving a 
deliverable to a later revision would be a Group decision. The cost is 
that the Group's deliverables would rest on a single-author draft that 
I edit, that has no IETF standing, and that may change between 
revisions. Pinning each deliverable to a revision limits that 
dependency but does not remove it.

2. Hold open. The charter records that the normative reference is 
unsettled until a document with IETF working-group or RFC standing 
exists. Under this option the use-case catalogue, conformance criteria 
written against the charter's scope, and a regulatory crosswalk at the 
level of capabilities can proceed, but the baseline profile and the 
test vectors, which need a protocol text to cite, would wait.

The proposed v1.1 text below implements option 1. Its other changes:

- §2 and §4 update the element list for -03: signed receipts with an audit
  log, and an erasure operation with two stated erasure classes.
- §2.1 records that the v1.0 restriction lapsed when the Independent
  Submission consideration ended on 25 July without publication as an RFC.
  Normative re-specification of the protocol stays out of scope under §4.
- §5 drops the §2.1 condition on later work.
- §6 updates the IETF entry. v1.0 said AAIF hosts the reference
  implementation; AAIF hosts the Model Context Protocol, and the entry now
  says so.
- §7, §11 and §12 are tidied to match. Scope (§3) is unchanged. §13 lists the
  changes section by section.

Section 11 asks for a stated review period. I am setting two weeks, so 
this call closes on 2 November 2026. Please reply on this list, whether 
you support option 1, prefer option 2, or would rather the Group 
reference another document. I will answer each reply here. If the 
replies favour option 2 or another document, I will withdraw this text 
and circulate a v1.1 for that choice with a new two-week review period. 
I will count a reply preferring option 2 or another document as an 
objection to option 1. If there is no sustained objection to option 1 
when the call closes, I will confirm the result on this list. If there 
is one, option 1 does not carry, and I will set out next steps here.

Because this concerns my own draft, anyone who would prefer that 
another participant assess the outcome can say so and propose who, and 
I will abide by that participant's assessment. If the Group cannot 
agree on someone, I will ask the W3C Community Development Lead, who 
handles escalation for Community Groups, to assess it or to name 
someone who will. A formal objection can be recorded under §9, item 3.

The full text of the proposed v1.1 follows below.

Regards,
Russell Jackson
Chair, AI Agent Memory Interoperability Community Group

======================================================================== 
Proposed charter v1.1 (full text) 
========================================================================

Charter — AI Agent Memory Interoperability Community Group (v1.1)

Group: AI Agent Memory Interoperability Community Group
Group home: https://www.w3.org/community/ai-agent-memory-interop/
Short name: ai-agent-memory-interop
Status: v1.1 — Proposed (call for consensus circulated 18 October 2026)
Adopted: v1.0 on 2026-06-19 (call for consensus circulated 2026-06-05; 
carried absent sustained objection per §9)

This Group elects to adopt a charter — optional under the W3C Community 
Group process — to fix its scope before technical work begins. The v1.0 
charter was adopted by Group consensus on 19 June 2026. Version 1.1 is 
proposed under §11. It changes §2, §2.1, §4, §5, §6, §7, §11 and §12 to 
describe the current status of the referenced specification and to 
record that the v1.0 restriction has lapsed. Scope (§3) is unchanged, 
and §4 keeps the same out-of-scope items. The changes are listed in 
§13.

1. Goals

The mission of the AI Agent Memory Interoperability Community Group is 
to make AI agent memory portable and verifiable across vendors, models, 
agent frameworks, and tool ecosystems by developing, in the open and 
royalty-free:

- cross-vendor interoperability profiles,
- a documented use-case catalogue, and
- a conformance and test surface (criteria, test vectors, and a test suite)

that build upon and normatively reference an externally-maintained 
protocol specification for AI agent memory, rather than re-specifying 
that protocol within this Group.

The Group operates one layer above the protocol. Its purpose is to help 
independent implementations interoperate and to let adopters verify 
conformance — not to define, fork, or maintain a second copy of the 
underlying protocol.

2. Referenced protocol specification

The normative definition of the AI agent memory protocol — the 
memory-cell shape and encoding, the cryptographic envelope and key 
derivation, the identity/signature binding, the signed receipts and 
audit log, the sharing contracts, and the erasure operation with its 
stated erasure classes — is maintained outside this Group, in the 
individual Internet-Draft draft-saihm-memory-protocol 
(https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-saihm-memory-protocol/). The 
current revision is draft-saihm-memory-protocol-03. An Internet-Draft 
is a work in progress; this one has not been adopted by an IETF working 
group and is not an IETF standard.

All Group deliverables reference that document normatively, and each 
deliverable names the revision it was written against. Moving a 
deliverable to a later revision is a Group decision under §9. Where 
this charter names a protocol element (e.g. an ML-DSA-65 identity 
binding or a per-cell data-encryption key), it does so only to scope 
interoperability and conformance guidance for the element as defined in 
the referenced specification — never to redefine it.

2.1 Relationship to the IETF (history of the v1.0 restriction)

Charter v1.0 restricted the Group from developing a competing normative 
specification while draft-saihm-memory-protocol was under consideration 
in the IETF Independent Submission Stream. That consideration concluded 
on 25 July 2026 without publication as an RFC, when the document was 
moved out of the stream, and the restriction lapsed by its own terms. 
The current revision, -03, is an individual Internet-Draft. Its header 
still names the Independent Submission stream as the intended stream, 
but it is not under the Independent Submissions Editor's consideration.

§4 continues to place normative re-specification of the protocol out of 
scope, and changing that requires an amendment under §11. If the Group 
intends to take on normative work, it will coordinate with the editor 
of the referenced specification (currently the Chair) before doing so.

3. Scope

In scope:

- Interoperability profiles that constrain or clarify use of the referenced
  protocol for specific deployment contexts (for example: a baseline
  cross-vendor interoperability profile; a profile for regulated-data
  deployments), adding interoperability guidance without adding new normative
  protocol elements.
- A use-case catalogue capturing how portability, verifiable erasure, and
  cross-vendor migration are exercised in practice.
- Conformance criteria, test vectors, and a test suite by which an
  implementation can demonstrate conformance to the referenced specification
  and to the Group's profiles.
- A regulatory crosswalk pack (informative): mappings from the referenced
  protocol's capabilities to GDPR Article 17, the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU)
  2024/1689), NIST AI RMF 1.0, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, ISO/IEC 42001:2023, and
  comparable regimes (HIPAA, PIPEDA, PIPL, LGPD).
- Liaison and coordination notes with the forums listed in §6.

4. Out of scope

- Normative (re-)specification of the AI agent memory protocol itself. The
  cell format and encoding, the cryptographic envelope and key derivation, the
  identity/signature binding, the signed receipts and audit log, the sharing
  contracts, and the erasure operation are defined normatively only in the
  referenced specification (§2); this Group references them and does not
  redefine, version, or fork them.
- Selection or endorsement of any specific blockchain or ledger (the
  referenced protocol is chain-agnostic).
- Vector-database or embedding-store semantics (orthogonal to memory
  portability).
- Agent runtime semantics (addressed in other forums).
- Tool-routing or tool-invocation semantics (addressed by the Model Context
  Protocol and its host foundation).

5. Deliverables

Each deliverable is a Community Group Report (or test software) that 
normatively references draft-saihm-memory-protocol at a named revision. 
None re-specifies the protocol.

First 12 months (target):

1. Use-case catalogue — portability, verifiable erasure, cross-vendor 
migration, regulated-data handling. 2. Baseline interoperability 
profile — the minimum interoperability guidance two independent 
implementations need to exchange and verify memory cells per the 
referenced specification. 3. Conformance and test-vector pack — 
conformance criteria plus published test vectors for the referenced 
protocol's encoding, identity binding, envelope, and erasure-receipt 
verification. 4. Regulatory crosswalk pack (informative) — the mappings 
enumerated in §3.

Candidate later work (subject to participant interest):

- A regulated-data interoperability profile.
- An open-source conformance test suite exercising the test vectors.

The Group expects to publish work as it stabilises and to maintain an 
errata/issues list against each Report.

5.1 Normative status / limitation

Per the W3C process, a Community Group does not produce W3C 
Recommendations or other W3C standards. Group deliverables are 
Community Group Reports. A Report may, if the Group elects, be advanced 
to a Final Specification under the W3C Community Final Specification 
Agreement; this provides patent commitments but is still not a W3C 
Recommendation. Placing any work on the W3C Recommendation track would 
require transitioning the work to a W3C Working Group under a separate, 
Member-reviewed charter. This charter does not commit W3C or its 
Members to any such transition.

6. Dependencies and liaisons

- IETF — the referenced protocol is maintained as an individual Internet-Draft
  (§2), this Group's primary upstream dependency. The Group tracks new
  revisions and aligns its profiles and test vectors to them. The Group also
  follows the IETF Agent Communication Protocols (agentproto) Working Group,
  chartered on 8 October 2026, for adjacent work. That charter states that the
  dialog context it covers "does not include the content exchanged with AI
  models, such as conversation memory, retrieved documents, or prompts."
- Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF, Linux Foundation) — hosts the Model Context
  Protocol. The referenced specification defines a tool binding for it.
- NIST — the AI RMF and related guidance, engaged via use-case and
  public-comment contributions; informative crosswalk only.
- ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 — a possible future track via national-body engagement;
  this Group's output is intended to be cite-able by an SC 42 New Work Item
  Proposal should SC 42 choose to engage. No commitment is implied.

7. Community Group Process and contribution mechanics

This Group operates under the W3C Community and Business Group Process 
and the W3C Community Contributor License Agreement (CLA):

- Anyone may participate by creating a W3C account, joining the Group at
  https://www.w3.org/community/ai-agent-memory-interop/join, and agreeing to
  the CLA.
- Substantive contributions to a specification are made under the CLA. Should
  the Group take a specification to a Final Specification, participants commit
  under the W3C Community Final Specification Agreement (royalty-free patent
  commitments).
- The Group's IPR mode is Royalty-Free.
- All technical work — drafts, issues, decisions, and minutes — is conducted
  in public.

8. Communication

- Group home: https://www.w3.org/community/ai-agent-memory-interop/
- Public mailing list: the Group's W3C-hosted public list
  (public-ai-agent-memory-interop@w3.org).
- Source and issues: a public Git repository linked from the Group home once
  established.
- Meetings (if any): announced on the list with public minutes; meetings are
  not a substitute for on-list decision-making.

9. Decision process

The Group seeks consensus. Where a decision is needed:

1. The Chair calls for consensus on the list or in a minuted meeting. 
2. Absent sustained objection, the proposal carries. 3. A participant 
who disagrees may record a formal objection; the Chair responds on the 
record and, where warranted, escalates per the W3C Community Group 
process.

Decisions made in a meeting are confirmed on the public list so that 
participants who could not attend can weigh in. Substantive decisions 
are not made off-list.

10. Chair / convener

The Group is convened and chaired by Russell Jackson (W3C handle saihm, 
W3C user 177015 — https://www.w3.org/users/177015/).

The convener serves as initial Chair. The Group is vendor-neutral and 
open to all implementers; participation and membership do not imply 
endorsement of any implementation, including any maintained by the 
Chair. The Group may select or add Chairs by the decision process in 
§9. Group contact is via the public mailing list (§8).

11. Amendments to this charter

This charter may be amended by the decision process in §9. Proposed 
amendments are circulated on the public list with at least a stated 
review period before adoption. The charter version and date are updated 
on each adopted amendment.

12. References

- W3C Community and Business Group Process:
  https://www.w3.org/community/about/process/
- W3C Community Contributor License Agreement (CLA):
  https://www.w3.org/community/about/process/cla/
- W3C Community Final Specification Agreement (FSA):
  https://www.w3.org/community/about/process/final/
- IETF Internet-Draft (referenced protocol):
  https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-saihm-memory-protocol/ (current
  revision: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-saihm-memory-protocol/03/)
- IETF agentproto Working Group charter:
  https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/charter-ietf-agentproto/
- NIST FIPS-204 (ML-DSA): https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final

13. Changes from v1.0

- §2: the referenced document is described as an individual Internet-Draft,
  with its current revision named. The list of protocol elements follows that
  revision (signed receipts and audit log; an erasure operation with stated
  erasure classes), and the example element "HKDF-derived per-cell key"
  becomes "per-cell data-encryption key". Deliverables name the revision they
  were written against, and moving to a later revision is a Group decision.
- §2.1: the Independent Submission restriction is recorded as lapsed on 25
  July 2026. The commitment to coordinate with the editor of the referenced
  specification before taking on normative work is kept, and the editor is
  named as currently the Chair.
- §4: the element names follow §2, and the pointer to the §2.1 restriction is
  removed. The out-of-scope items are the same.
- §5: deliverables reference the specification at a named revision, and the
  §2.1 condition on later work is removed.
- §6: the IETF entry is updated. v1.0 said AAIF hosts the reference
  implementation; the entry now says that AAIF hosts the Model Context
  Protocol.
- §7: the v1.0 line giving the license of the reference implementation is
  removed. The Group's IPR mode is unchanged.
- §11: the cross-reference to the lapsed restriction is removed.
- §12: links to the current revision and to the agentproto charter are added.
