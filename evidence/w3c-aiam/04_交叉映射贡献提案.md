## 1. What this is, and what it is not

This is a **method plus seven worked rows**, not a finished crosswalk pack. It exists to answer one question the charter leaves open: when §3 says the crosswalk maps "the referenced protocol's capabilities" to named regimes "and comparable regimes", what is the unit of a mapping, and what makes a mapping checkable by someone who was not in the room?

It is not a legal opinion, not a conformance claim about any implementation, and not a proposal to change §3. It uses the instrument test the Chair recorded as his reading of §3 on 23 November 2026:

> "any law, regulation or standard that sets requirements for how records are kept, protected, retained, corrected or erased."

I split that sentence into axes because "comparable" is doing five different jobs in it, and a crosswalk that treats them as one job will produce rows nobody can check.

## 2. Method

**Step 1 — the instrument test, applied to an instrument, not a jurisdiction.** A named instrument enters the set only if it can be pointed to by number and date. A process (such as a registration review) enters only when the phrase is recorded as a process, per the Chair's recorded reading.

**Step 2 — decompose the obligation into five axes.** Keeping, protecting, retaining, correcting, erasing. An obligation that looks singular in the instrument is usually two axes joined by a connector, and the two halves often get different answers. Rows below are split wherever the connector was doing work.

**Step 3 — anchor each axis to an element of -03.** Not to "the protocol" in general. The anchorable surface in -03 is finite: the cell tuple and `cellId` recomputation (§2.1), the holder identity binding and KEY receipt (§2.2), the receipt set and audit-log lookup keys (§2.5), KEK versioning and DEK re-wrapping (§2.4), the three sharing modes and revocation (§2.6), the two erasure classes (§2.7), the storage tier with "stated durability and retention properties" (§1.4), and the eight MCP tools (§4).

**Step 4 — four-part determination, one row each.** Every row states: (a) the obligation, cited to instrument and paragraph; (b) the anchored element; (c) one of three determinations — **satisfied / not satisfied / not assessable**; (d) the **evidence read-point** — where a third party reads the fact that supports the determination.

Three conventions, chosen so the output can be reproduced rather than believed:

- **Not assessable is never a pass.** It means the format offers no read-point, and it is recorded as a gap against the pinned revision. This is the convention already in use in the conformance reporting under discussion, where a non-exercised criterion is kept separate from pass and fail.
- **A determination is about the format, not about a deployment.** "Satisfied" means a conformant operator *can* demonstrate the fact from the protocol surface, not that any particular operator does.
- **The instrument's own second-order references are recorded as such.** Where an instrument says "as required by other law", the row is not assessable until that other law is named, and the row names it.

China's Art. 46 matters more than its length suggests: it is the only instrument in the set that addresses *electronic* records as such, and it puts the signature requirement one level out, by referring to other law. A crosswalk that skips it would be mapping the wrong layer.

## 4. Fifteen worked rows

Determination vocabulary: S = satisfied, NS = not satisfied, NA = not assessable (gap against the pinned revision).

Reading the column: **four satisfied (one narrowly — R2 covers overwrite-resistance, not persistence), seven not satisfied, four not assessable.** The gaps are not defects in -03 — the charter's own §3 says a profile may add interoperability guidance but not new protocol elements, and a gap that needs a new element stays recorded as a gap. That is exactly why they should be recorded now rather than discovered by the first regulated adopter.

## 5. What the rows imply, stated as questions rather than demands

1. **Carriage versus custody.** If a retention horizon is to be checkable from the format rather than from an operator's prose, something in the protocol surface has to carry it — the cell tuple, the tier declaration, or a governance record. The Group may prefer to leave it in the profile. If so, the useful sentence is not "the tier states its retention" but "the tier declaration is a named, versioned document whose absence makes R1 not assessable".
2. **Supersession as a first-class relation.** R2 is satisfiable today and R3 is not, because the format preserves the old cell but does not describe the relation between old and new. A supersession link would make both assessable without adding a normative protocol element — it is interoperability guidance.
3. **Two signatures are doing one job.** In the custodial profile, "who wrote this" and "who vouches for this store" are the same signature. Any regulated deployment that has to attribute an act to a person will notice. Worth an explicit statement in the profile, even if the answer is "out of scope for -03".
4. **The boundary record.** R8 is the row I would most like the Group to disagree with me about, because if there is a cheaper way to represent "retained under obligation X until Y", it changes the shape of the pack.

## 6. Relationship to the measurement work already on the list

This method and the benchmarking method under discussion answer different questions and neither replaces the other. The benchmarking method measures what a store *does* when memory is edited beneath it — detection, at read time or audit time. This method asks what the format *says* a store must be able to show. A row here can be satisfied while a store still fails a detection test, and the reverse. Where they meet is the reporting vocabulary: "not exercised" and "not assessable" should not be allowed to drift into two different meanings in two documents.

## 7. Limits

- Fifteen rows are an illustration, not a pack. A pack covering the §3 regimes in the same granularity is a larger piece of work, and I would bring it section by section rather than at once.
- Every legal reading here is mine. Where an instrument delegates to other law, the row says so rather than resolving it.
- No measurement is claimed in this document, and no implementation is named.
- If any reading above is wrong, a citation to the paragraph that says otherwise is all the correction I need.
